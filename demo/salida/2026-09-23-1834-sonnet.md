fn intersect(xs: List[Int], ys: List[Int]) -> List[Int]
  requires len(xs) >= 0
  ensures forall x in result: contains(ys, x)
  ensures forall x in xs: not contains(ys, x) or count(result, x) == count(xs, x)
  ensures forall x in xs: contains(ys, x) or count(result, x) == 0

**Report**

`intersect` is stored (hash `38ab8fe814e2`) with certificate **level 1**: the 4 examples passed, but the prover did not close its proof.

- **Body:** it recurses on `xs`. It keeps the head `h` if `contains_in(ys, h)` and drops it otherwise, so order and repetitions from `xs` are kept.
- **Contract:**
  - `forall x in result: contains(ys, x)`: everything returned is in `ys`.
  - `forall x in xs: not contains(ys, x) or count(result, x) == count(xs, x)`: elements of `xs` that are in `ys` keep all their repetitions.
  - `forall x in xs: contains(ys, x) or count(result, x) == 0`: elements not in `ys` are dropped.
  - `len(result) <= len(xs)`.
- **Dependency:** `sello_deps` shows `contains_in` (`cebf92985dd7`). I read only its signature and contract, `result == contains(xs, v)`.
- **`sello_check` calls:** 4.
- **Errors:**
  - **E401:** `contains_in` was unknown when I called it from `intersect`. Functions in the store can't be called from a source file, and `sello_add` gave the same error. I put a copy of `contains_in` in the source. Its hash matched the stored one (`cached: true`), so the store's version is the one used.
  - **Prover timeout:** `intersect` stayed at level 1 in all three variants I checked, with "timeout: the solver did not answer". The variants were the full contract, a `contains(ys, x) == (count(result, x) == count(xs, x))` form, and a reduced contract with only membership and length. The obligation the solver could not decide is the postcondition after `[h] ++ intersect(t, ys)`: `count` and `forall` over a concatenated list. I couldn't get around it.
- **Certificate levels:**
  - `intersect`: level 1.
  - The `contains_in` copy proved at level 2 in `sello_check`. The stored `contains_in` still shows level 1, because its certificate was cached.

The contract doesn't state order directly. It fixes which elements appear and how many times, so a wrong body would break it, but a result with the right elements in the wrong order would pass. The examples cover order.
